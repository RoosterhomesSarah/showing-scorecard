# Start here

You have this folder open. That is the whole setup.

Say this and it will take it from there:

> set me up

It asks seven questions about your business, takes about three minutes, and then builds
your first scorecard with you. You will not need to open another file.

---

## What you are going to have in ten minutes

A link you text to a buyer. They open it on their phone while standing in a house and
score it against **their own list**, the one they gave you in their consultation. Not a
generic checklist.

Two people score separately on the same phone, so when he counts the yard and she
doesn't, it shows up right there instead of six weeks later. A deal-breaker takes a
house out no matter how well it scored. The big systems get logged on their own, because
a house can tick every box they asked for and still need a thirty thousand dollar sewer
line.

At the end they tap one button and send you the whole thing.

---

## The seven questions

So you know what is coming. Nothing here is hard and none of it is permanent.

1. Your business name, as it should read on the page
2. Your first name, the way a client says it
3. Two colors, as hex codes if you have them. Say "I don't know" and you get a sensible
   pair you can change later
4. Your logo, if you have a file. Skip it if you don't
5. The phone number a client should text you on
6. The email a client should send results to
7. One line for the footer, usually your business and your website

Answers go in `your-brand.json`. It stays on your computer.

## Then, for each buyer

Say who you are touring with:

> build a tour scorecard for the Hendersons

**If you did a buyer consultation**, point it at your notes. A photo of a handwritten
intake sheet works. That is the best result and the fastest.

**If you didn't**, it asks you eight questions about that buyer and takes three minutes.
You get the scorecard, and you also get a consultation on file that you did not have
before. Next tour with them starts from it.

**If they are already in your car**, it starts from a template for their buyer type and
says so, then asks you one question after the first house to sharpen it into their real
list.

One client, or twelve. Each one gets their own folder under `clients/` and their own
scorecard. Nothing collides.

---

## A thing worth knowing before your first one

The whole method is to take what your buyer said and score houses against it. A scorecard
scores properties, so anything they said that describes the people in an area rather than
the house itself gets converted before it lands on the page.

"Good schools." "A safe neighborhood." "A family area." Buyers say these constantly.
"Good schools" becomes the named attendance boundary. "Safe" becomes a link to the city's
own crime map rather than a score. The tool tells you what it changed and why, each time,
so after a few tours you catch them yourself.

What you end up with is the same objective criteria, written down, applied to every
client and every property. Run your criteria past your broker before your first one, and
work from your own state association's current advisory. This tool is not legal advice
and neither is anything in it.
